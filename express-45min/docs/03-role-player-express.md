# ROLE PLAYER BRIEFING: The Owner (Express, 45-Minute Edition)
### "The Friday Counterfeit" · Bleu Hen Hospitality Group

**Your role:** **Dana**, Owner and founder of Bleu Hen Hospitality Group.
**Your job:** Be a stressed, broke-if-wrong, time-starved executive and pressure-test two teams in about 18 minutes total. You appear **three times**: the Gauntlet (twice) and the Owner's Call.
**Read first:** `../../docs/03-role-player-briefing.md` for the full persona and private backstory. This is the compressed version.

---

## 1. Persona in 30 Seconds

- **Highly stressed. Financially panicked. Time-starved. Bottom line only.**
- No patience for PR fluff, hedging or reading from a script.
- Not cruel; desperate. You respect clarity, honesty and a straight answer.

**Private pressure (use it; never reveal an answer):**
- You personally guaranteed a **$1.8 million loan.** Your house is on it.
- Teague is your friend. He came to your 10-year party.
- You noticed the November price drop and told your accountant it was great news.
- Servers like Mateo (sick kid) live on Friday and Saturday tips.

**Voice:** "Bottom line." "How much?" "Say that again, shorter." "I built this on a sentence: seventy-five miles."

---

## 2. Rules of Engagement

1. **Interrupt within 15 seconds.** The pitch is only 2 minutes.
2. **Cut off anyone reading.** "Stop reading. Look at me."
3. **Force eye contact.**
4. **Cold-call at least two non-spokespeople** during your 6:30 of questions: "You. Who wrote point two?"
5. **Ask each team the same core questions** (Section 4). Both teams get at least **4**.
6. **Never say what you would do** or reveal a "right" answer.
7. **Reward honest cost** by softening a notch. Punish vagueness by tightening.
8. **Stay in character** until the facilitator says "Breaking character."

---

## 3. Timing for Each Team (8.5 minutes)

| Time | Your move |
|---|---|
| **0:00 to 0:15** | Interrupt: **"Bottom line. What are we doing?"** |
| **0:15 to 2:00** | Pitch continues. Cut in at the weakest point |
| **2:00 to 6:30** | Questions. Run the pressure questions below. Cold-call two non-spokespeople |
| **6:30 to 8:30** | Last two: *"What's the one thing you need from me?"* and *"Read me your guest script."* |
| **Close** | "Thank you. Next." No verdict yet |

---

## 4. Pressure Questions (pick by strategy)

| If the team... | Ask |
|---|---|
| **Scraps the food** | **"Who is paying to replace the $40,000 in the walk-in right now?"** · "And the supplier says he'll sue me if I pull it. Who covers that?" |
| **Serves the food** | **"When the local news shows up with cameras at 6:00 PM, who is going on camera to defend this?"** · "If you're wrong, what happens to us Monday?" |
| **Reads a script** | **"Look me in the eye. Imagine I'm proposing to my girlfriend at table 4 tonight. Say that exact script to my face."** |
| **Hedges or waits** | **"It's 3:30. The article drops in ninety minutes. Which one is it? Pick one."** |
| **Ignores the lawsuit** | **"The supplier's lawyer just said he'd sue me into the ground if I touch the product. Where is that in your plan?"** |
| **Has contradictory deliverables** | **"Your memo says one thing, your chef point says another, and your server script says a third. Which one is real?"** |
| **Anyone** | "How much? Give me a number." · "What do my servers say at 5:01?" |

**Optional curveball (if you have time):** At about 4 minutes into questioning, say: *"My GM just texted. TV crew at the front door. Do I go out? What do I say?"*

---

## 5. The Owner's Call (1 minute, at 38:00)

In character, to the room:
1. **Which plan (or which parts)** you would act on right now, in bottom-line terms.
2. **One thing each team failed to answer.**

Don't say who "won." If they chose the same strategy, compare **execution:** whose numbers held up, whose deliverables matched, whose people could answer cold.

**Example:** *"I'm going with Team 1's call to pull the product. They gave me the real cost and every person in the room could back it up. Team 2, you never told me who's on camera at 6:00. Move."*

---

## 6. Boundaries

- Professional tone. No insults, profanity or personal attacks.
- If someone is genuinely distressed, soften: "Take a breath. Your first sentence."
- Don't leak the hidden truth. If asked "was it real?": "That's what I'm asking you."
