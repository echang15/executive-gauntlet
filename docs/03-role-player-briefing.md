# ROLE PLAYER BRIEFING: "The Restaurant Group Owner"

**Your role:** The Owner of a three-location, farm-to-table restaurant group.
**Your job:** Pressure-test four teams of students as they pitch you, in character, in a crisis. You are the "executive judge."
**You are NOT:** A villain, a lecturer or a source of the right answer. You never reveal a preferred strategy.

---

## 1. The Situation From Your Point of View

It's **2:00 PM on Friday of Valentine's Day weekend**. All three restaurants are fully booked. Projected weekend revenue is **$200,000**.

Your "local, sustainable" supplier may be a fraud, and a journalist is publishing at **5:00 PM**. Your coolers hold **$40,000** of the product. **You paid for it. You built the brand on it. You marketed it.**

At about **3:05 PM**, you learned that the supplier is **denying everything** and threatening **defamation and breach-of-contract penalties** if you pull their product. You have no proof either way.

You have a task force of students who are supposed to tell you what to do. You have **very little time**, and you need an answer.

---

## 2. Persona

| Trait | How it sounds and looks |
|---|---|
| **Highly stressed** | Fast, clipped speech. Checking your watch or phone. Rubbing your face. |
| **Financially panicked** | You think in dollars, always. "What does that cost?" is your default question. |
| **Time-starved** | You have a thousand other calls. "Get to the point." "Bottom line." |
| **Bottom-line focused** | Revenue, cost, liability, who pays. Brand talk only matters when it turns into money. |
| **Zero patience for fluff** | PR language, jargon, hedging and "we feel that" phrases make you visibly irritated. |
| **Not cruel** | You're desperate, not mean. You respect clarity, honesty and a straight answer. |

### Voice examples
- "Stop. Bottom line."
- "I don't have time for background. What are we doing?"
- "How much? Give me a number."
- "That's a lot of words. What do you want me to do?"
- "Okay. Say that again, but shorter."

### Emotional arc (use judgment)
- **Start tense, impatient.**
- If a team gives you a **clear BLUF with real numbers**, soften slightly. Lean in and say "Okay, go on."
- If a team **hedges, reads or dodges**, escalate your impatience.
- If a team **earns your trust** (honest about cost, clear ask), end with a firm but fair response, such as "Fine. I'll back that. Move."
- If a team **fails to**, end with "I'm not convinced. I need to think." Never humiliate them.

---

## 3. Rules of Engagement

1. **Interrupt early.** Within the first 20 to 30 seconds. Do not wait for them to finish their opening.
2. **Cut off anyone reading from a script.** Say, "Stop reading. Look at me." Wait. Make them restart in their own words.
3. **Force eye contact.** If a spokesperson is staring at notes or the floor, say their name or "Look at me, not the paper."
4. **Interrupt often but not constantly.** Roughly every 20 to 40 seconds. Let them land a full sentence now and then, so the interruptions mean something.
5. **Make them answer.** If they dodge, repeat the question: "That didn't answer it. Who? How much? When?"
6. **Reward directness.** When they give you a number or a clear call, acknowledge it briefly ("Okay.") and keep moving.
7. **Use the mid-point inject.** The supplier lawsuit threat is on the table. Make teams explain how it affects their plan.
8. **Stay in character the entire time** until the facilitator breaks it at 70:00.
9. **Be consistent across teams.** Ask each team at least 3 pressure questions. Don't go easy on a team because they're friendly.
10. **Never give the "right answer."** Don't say "You should have done X."
11. **Don't demean individuals.** Challenge the logic and delivery, not the person.

### Boundaries (for safety and learning)
- Keep a professional tone: no insults, profanity or personal attacks.
- If a student seems genuinely distressed (not just nervous), soften and say, "Take a breath. Give me your first sentence again." The facilitator may also signal a reset.
- Time discipline: the facilitator will chime at 3:00 and hard stop at about 6:00 per team. Wrap up when signaled.

---

## 4. Pitch Flow: What You Do

| Time into pitch | Your move |
|---|---|
| **0:00 to 0:30** | Interrupt early with a framing question: "Bottom line. What are we doing?" |
| **0:30 to 2:00** | Probe the **weakest point** in their strategy using the pressure questions below. |
| **2:00 to 3:00** | Challenge cost, execution or communications. Check for eye contact and script reading. |
| **3:00 to ~5:30** | Q&A. Make them commit: "Last question: what's the one thing you need from me right now?" |
| **Close** | Give a short in-character verdict (one or two sentences). |

---

## 5. Pressure Questions

Read the team's strategy as they pitch, then choose the matching question(s). **Use at least 3 per team.** Stay in character and push back on weak answers with a follow-up.

### Q1: If they SCRAP the food (pull the product, cancel or re-plan service)
> **"Who is paying to replace the $40,000 in the walk-in right now?"**

*Follow-ups:*
- "And the supplier says they'll sue me if I pull it. Who covers that?"
- "What are we serving 300 guests tonight? Do we even have anything to cook?"
- "How many reservations are we canceling, and what does that cost?"

*What a strong answer includes:* a specific number, a source of replacement product, an insurance or legal angle, and an honest cost of the choice.

### Q2: If they SERVE the food (keep the product on the menu)
> **"When the local news shows up with cameras at 6:00 PM, who is going on camera to defend this?"**

*Follow-ups:*
- "If you're wrong, and these guys really are a fraud, what happens to us on Monday?"
- "My server tells a guest it's 'local.' Is that a lie? Whose name is on it?"
- "How many guests are going to take a picture of their plate and post it?"

*What a strong answer includes:* a named spokesperson, a plan for changing or qualifying the menu language, and a clear-eyed view of the risk.

### Q3: If they read a robotic PR script
> **"Look me in the eye. Imagine I'm proposing to my girlfriend at table 4 tonight. Say that exact script to my face."**

*Follow-ups:*
- "Would you say that to your own mother? Try again."
- "That sounded like a press release. What would a real person say?"
- "Is it true? Then why does it sound fake?"

*What a strong answer includes:* a human, honest, short statement spoken naturally, without a notes-page crutch.

### Q4: If they HEDGE or "wait and see" (no clear decision)
> **"It's 3:30. The article drops in ninety minutes. Which one is it: serve or scrap? Pick one."**

*Follow-ups:*
- "'Monitor the situation' isn't a plan. What do we do at 4:59?"
- "If I wait, what exactly do I learn between now and then?"

*What a strong answer includes:* a decision, a trigger for changing it, and acknowledgement that waiting is itself a choice.

### Q5: If they IGNORE or gloss over the supplier lawsuit threat
> **"The supplier's lawyer just said they'd sue me into the ground if I touch their product. Where is that in your plan?"**

*Follow-ups:*
- "How much is that penalty? You don't know? Then how do you know your plan is cheaper?"
- "Did anyone call counsel? Who, and what did they say?"
- "Can we say we're 'pausing' the product instead of 'pulling' it? Is that honest?"

*What a strong answer includes:* explicit treatment of legal exposure, wording choices that avoid accusation without being deceptive, and a request for counsel.

### Q6: If their three deliverables CONTRADICT each other (BLUF vs. Chef vs. guest script)
> **"Your memo says one thing, your Chef instructions say another, and your server script says a third. Which one is real?"**

*Follow-ups:*
- "My Chef is going to read point two and walk out of the kitchen. What does he do first?"
- "Your server just promised a guest something your kitchen can't deliver."

*What a strong answer includes:* ownership of the inconsistency and a quick fix, not defensiveness.

### Bonus questions (use when time allows or a team is strong)
- **On honesty:** "Do we tell guests the truth about what happened, or do we wait for the article?"
- **On staff:** "My servers are about to be asked about this at every table. What do I tell them, and who's training them in the next hour?"
- **On cost:** "I can afford to lose $40,000 or $200,000 but not both. Which one do I protect, and why?"
- **On the ask:** "I have one decision to make in the next ten minutes. What is it?"

## 6. Reading the Room: Quick Reference

| If the team... | Then you... |
|---|---|
| Opens with a clear BLUF | Nod once, say "Okay." Go straight to cost or lawsuit. |
| Buries the lead | Interrupt: "Stop. What's the recommendation?" |
| Reads from paper | "Stop reading. Look at me." Make them restart. |
| Gives vague numbers | "How much? Give me a number." |
| Admits uncertainty honestly | Respect it. "Okay. What's your best guess?" |
| Overstates certainty | "How do you know that? You have no proof." |
| Argues with you | Hold your ground, then ask, "Is that your answer?" |
| Freezes | Wait 3 seconds, then say, "Take your time. First sentence." |
| Recovers well after an interruption | Reward it: "Good. Continue." |

---

## 7. Closing Each Pitch

At the end of Q&A, give a short in-character verdict. Keep it to 1 to 2 sentences, **non-evaluative about "right answer," focused on your gut as the Owner.** Examples:

- **Strong:** "Fine. You're clear and you gave me the real cost. I'll make the call. Go."
- **Mixed:** "I like parts of it, but I'm still worried about the lawsuit. We'll talk."
- **Weak:** "I'm not there yet. I don't have an answer I can act on."

Then stay quiet and let the facilitator move on.

---

## 8. After the Session

When the facilitator says **"Breaking character,"** step out of persona right away. You may be asked to share one or two observations in the debrief:
- Which team gave you the **clearest BLUF**?
- Who handled interruptions best?
- What moment made you trust or distrust a team?

Keep feedback specific and behavior-based (what they said and did), not personal.

## Pre-Session Checklist

- [ ] Read this briefing twice, including all six pressure questions.
- [ ] Meet the facilitator 15 minutes early; agree on the **reset signal**.
- [ ] Have a watch or phone in hand (props help the persona).
- [ ] Decide your **opening line** (e.g., "I've got ten minutes. Go.").
- [ ] Remember: **interrupt early, don't let anyone read, make them look at you.**
