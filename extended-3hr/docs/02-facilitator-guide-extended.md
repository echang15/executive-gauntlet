# FACILITATOR'S GUIDE: Extended (3-Hour) Edition
### "The Friday Counterfeit" · Bleu Hen Hospitality Group

> **Read first:** `../../docs/00-scenario-bible.md`, then `00-extended-scenario-addendum.md`. The addendum holds the contract, the inventory, the hidden truth and the Article.
> **Audience:** The lead facilitator. The Owner reads `03-role-player-briefing-extended.md`. Desk staff read `04-resource-desk-playbook.md`.

**Format:** 180 minutes, almost real-time. **No teaching is built in.** Teams arrive already taught BLUF, triage and pitch structure. If they weren't, hand them the one-page refresher on the Team Worksheet and warn them they're on their own.
**Participants:** 20 students in 2 teams of 10 (adaptations for 3 or 4 teams in Section 14).
**Goal:** Sustained pressure. Teams must decide with too much, too little and conflicting information while being interrupted, then own the words they put on the record.

## 2. Room Setup and Materials

**Layout** (see the sketch)

```
 [ Team A table (10) ]                       [ Team B table (10) ]

                     [ OWNER STATION ]
          [ DESK A ]                 [ DESK B ]
                  [ RUNNER / LEDGER ]
 [ STORY CLOCK ]      [ SESSION TIMER ]       (both visible from every table)
```

- **Two team tables**, far apart, so neither team overhears the other. Ten chairs each. A whiteboard or flip chart per table.
- **Resource Desk:** two small tables with a "Desk A" and a "Desk B" sign, a chair for callers, a desk phone prop if available, and a 3-minute timer. Desks must be **out of earshot** of the teams.
- **Owner Station:** a table at the front facing the room, with two chairs for the Gauntlet. Keep it a step away from the desks.
- **Two clocks, both visible to every table.**
  1. **Session timer** (counting up from 0:00 to 3:00).
  2. **Story Clock** (a big analog or digital clock showing story time: 2:00 PM at session 0:10). You will **reset it manually at 1:30** (to 3:35 PM) when the time skip happens.
- **Immersion props:** Lovebird menu (Exhibit D) on every table, a red flower per table, two phone-ring sound clips (for inbound calls), low music during Orientation that you stop at the Drop.

**Print list (per team)**
- 10 copies of the Extended Crisis Briefing
- 1 copy each of Exhibits A to E and G to N (the Dossier)
- 1 Team Worksheet (5 pages)
- 1 Call Log (Exhibit N, back side) and a stack of Call Slips (blank paper is fine)

**Print list (facilitator)**
- Event Cards (Run Sheet cards, cut apart and sorted by session time)
- The Article, printed large, for reading aloud
- The Aftermath Cards (Section 10)
- The Team Ledger (Section 9) and a Scorecard per team (Section 12)
- Two copies of the Main Inject card (E13), in sealed envelopes

**Pre-session checklist (arrive 45 minutes early)**
- [ ] Both clocks running and visible. Story Clock set to **1:50 PM**.
- [ ] Desks staffed, each holding their Playbook cards.
- [ ] Owner briefed. Agree on the **reset signal** (a raised hand and "Take a breath").
- [ ] Event cards sorted in order, with the Runner knowing the plan.
- [ ] Print the Article with blanks for each team's statement.
- [ ] Tell everyone: **we will interrupt you all day. That is the exercise.**

## 4. Orientation (0:00 – 0:10)

Teams are already seated. Music playing. Briefings face down.

**0:00 – 0:03: Frame.**
> "For the next three hours you are the Operational Task Force of Bleu Hen Hospitality Group. It's a real-time exercise. When the wall clock says 3:05, it's 3:05 in the story. You've been taught the tools. Today I won't teach you anything. I will interrupt you, mislead you accidentally and ask you to make decisions with bad information. You will be asked to put words on the record. Whatever you say will be used."

**0:03 – 0:06: Mechanics.**
- Explain the **Resource Desk** and **call tokens** (12 per team of 10). One caller at a time; 3 minutes per call.
- "Orders are binding. If you tell Chef Rafi to stop rolling product, he stops."
- "People will call *you*. They don't cost tokens, and they want an answer in 90 seconds."
- "Deliverables lock at 4:05. You'll hand in four."
- "At about 3:10 we take a ten-minute break. **The story clock will not stop.** Twenty-five minutes of story time will pass."

**0:06 – 0:08:** Name team captains (let teams choose). Confirm the roles in Section 6 of the Briefing are a suggestion.

**0:08 – 0:10: The read-aloud** (music off, lights low):
> "It is 1:55 PM, Friday, February thirteenth. In the kitchen at the Riverfront Bistro, the flounder is portioned. The roulade was rolled at noon. Eleven hundred and thirty guests are coming this weekend: first dates, anniversaries, a few marriage proposals. In five minutes you will learn that a reporter has put out a message saying your 'local, sustainable' supplier is a fraud, and that she has proof. She publishes at five. The doors open at five. Your Owner is waiting. Turn over your briefing."

At 0:10 sharp: **"The clock is running."** The Owner enters for the entrance speech.

## 5. The Owner's Entrance (0:10)

The Owner stands in the middle of the room for 60 seconds. Fast, clipped, frightened. Cover:
- The reporter, the 5:00 PM deadline, the $40,000 and $200,000.
- "I need a plan by 2:30. I need to know what we're serving tonight, and what I say if someone puts a camera in my face."
- "I'm going to check back on you. Don't make me chase you."

Then leave. The Owner returns at 0:40 for the Status Brief.

---

## 6. Phase 1: Intel Sweep (0:10 – 0:40)

**Purpose:** Teams read the dossier, make their first calls and form a first plan.

**What you do**
- Walk the room. **Do not coach.** Note who reads, who calls and who stays silent.
- Make sure the Runner delivers E02, E04, E06 and the Desk delivers E03, E05, E07 on time.
- Tell teams at 0:35 (2:25 PM): **"Status Brief in five minutes."**
- If a team hasn't made any call by 0:25, a Desk staffer walks over: **"Line's open."**

### Status Brief #1 (0:40 – 0:50)
- The Owner returns. **Each team gets a 4-minute slot:** 90 seconds spoken BLUF, then 2 minutes of questions and 30 seconds of transition.
- Draw order by coin flip. The other team stays at its table and keeps working, **quietly.**
- The Owner interrupts within 20 seconds. The Owner is looking for: a decision, a number and an ask. They do not give feedback; they say "Go work," and leave.

---

## 7. Phases 2 and 3 (0:50 – 1:55)

Follow the Run Sheet in Section 8 minute by minute. Between events:
- **Watch** for a team that has no plan to answer the next card. Do not rescue them.
- **Log** every instruction teams give to staff. The Runner keeps the Team Ledger (Section 9).
- **Escalate** if the room is calm: ring the call-in sound, send in the Chef, bring the Owner early. The exercise should never feel comfortable.

### The Time Skip (1:20 – 1:30)
At 1:20, after E15, say:
> "Break. Ten minutes. Leave your papers where they are. And understand this: **the clock does not stop.** While you're out, it will be 3:35."

Change the Story Clock from 3:10 to **3:35** while they are out of the room. When they return at 1:30, hand them E16, "While You Were Out." Let them read it in silence. **Do not extend any deadline.** Teams that planned to work through the break will find they're 25 minutes behind.

## 10. The Article and the Aftermath (2:36 – 2:46)

### 2:36 | Read the Article aloud
> *Stand. Printed copy in hand. Both teams seated together.* "It is 5:00 PM. The article has just published."

Read the Article from the Addendum (Section 9) **slowly**, with each team's statement inserted verbatim. If Team A and Team B differ, read **both versions**, labeled "Bleu Hen's statement" with the team's table letter. Include each conditional line that a team's ledger triggers:
- **Claimed no prior warning** → Tess quote.
- **Declined to comment** → "Bleu Hen declined to comment."
- **Scrubbed "local"** → web archive line.

Pause 10 seconds after reading each team's statement.

### 2:40 | Aftermath Cards
Read the card matching each team's **dominant path**. Name the table.

**Aftermath Card A: Pull everything, replace, disclose**
> "5:30 PM: The first seatings are thinner than planned; about thirty percent of tonight's covers have canceled. The kitchen is running a replacement menu that your guests mostly appreciate. 6:00 PM: Channel 6 quotes Bleu Hen as 'the first to act.' Saturday: Regulars fill the room out of loyalty. Monday: Teague's lawyers file against you under sections 12 and 14. Your lawyer files under section 15. The write-off is real. The brand survived."

**Aftermath Card B: Verification hold, serve beef, disclose**
> "5:30 PM: About twenty percent canceled. Guests who stay say they respect the honesty. 6:00 PM: Channel 6 calls it 'careful.' Saturday: Mostly full. Monday: Your written hold under section 9.4 is your strongest legal position. Teague's suit is weaker than he thought. The menu was thinner; the brand took a smaller hit."

**Aftermath Card C: Serve as planned, say nothing**
> "5:30 PM: A normal opening. Then servers start whispering. 6:00 PM: Channel 6 films plates being carried to tables. Saturday: Forty percent of reservations cancel. A server posts a video. Monday: If the allegation proves true, you are 'the ones who knew.' Your banker calls about the covenant."

**Aftermath Card D: Serve, with transparency**
> "5:30 PM: Twenty-five percent canceled. Tables are tense. 6:00 PM: Channel 6 says you're 'hedging.' Saturday: Full of the curious. Some guests leave mid-meal. Monday: Everything depends on what verification finds. If it clears Brandywine, you look brave. If not, you look slow."

**Aftermath Card E: Cancel all service**
> "5:30 PM: The doors are locked. One hundred and forty employees are on a text chain. 6:00 PM: Channel 6 films a dark restaurant. Saturday: Reopening unclear. Monday: Praised for integrity, mocked as panicked. The loan covenant is triggered. You will spend months rebuilding the group's cash."

## 11. The Gauntlet (2:00 – 2:36)

**Lock (1:55 – 2:00):** At 4:00 PM story time, say: **"Pens down."** Collect the four deliverables and Deliverable 4. Draw pitch order.

| Session | What | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 2:00 – 2:15 | **Team 1** (4:00 pitch, then about 11:00 of questions) | **Curveball C1 at 2:11** |
| 2:15 – 2:16 | Swap | Team 2 returns from the hallway or stays seated (see below) |
| 2:16 – 2:31 | **Team 2** | **Curveball C2 at 2:27** |
| 2:31 – 2:35 | **The Owner's Call** | Owner delivers decision, comments on both teams |
| 2:35 – 2:36 | Buffer | |

**Holding the second team:** If you have a hallway and a staff member, send Team 2 outside with instructions "you may rehearse but not change the deliverables." If not, the team sits silent at the back. The Owner does not reuse the same openers.

**During each pitch**
- Chime at 3:00 and 4:00 into the pitch (the pitch ends at 4:00).
- The Owner cold-calls team members, picks apart contradictions between deliverables, and uses the **expanded question bank** in the Role Player Briefing.
- **Hard stop at 15:00.** The Owner finishes the sentence in progress.
- Observe with the Scorecard (Section 12).

### The Owner's Call (2:31 – 2:35)
The Owner gives a **decision in character**: which plan (or parts) they will act on and why, what each team failed to answer, and what they're telling Priya for lineup. Keep it under 4 minutes. Do not grade.

## 12. Task Force Scorecard (private)

Score each team 0 to 3 on each criterion. **Use it for the debrief, not for ranking.** Share only what helps.

| # | Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Decision under uncertainty** | No decision | Decision with no stated assumptions | Decision with assumptions | Decision, assumptions, and a trigger for changing it |
| 2 | **BLUF clarity** | Buried | Late | Mostly first | Always first, repeated under interruption |
| 3 | **Cost honesty** | No numbers | Wrong numbers | Right numbers, buried | Right numbers, stated first |
| 4 | **Use of information** | Dossier ignored | Facts used loosely | Found a key fact (inventory, beef, contract) | Found contract §9.4 or the beef block and used it |
| 5 | **Handling interruptions** | Frozen | Answers, loses thread | Answers and returns | Uses them to strengthen the plan |
| 6 | **Integrity traps (T1 to T4)** | Fell for 3+ | Fell for 2 | Fell for 1 | Handled all, with counsel |
| 7 | **Consistency of the four deliverables** | Contradictions | One contradiction | Consistent, rough | Consistent and in the same voice |
| 8 | **Team function** | One person did it all | Spokespeople only | Most could answer | **Anyone could answer a cold call** |

## 13. Debrief (2:46 – 3:00)

**2:46: Break character.** The Owner and desk staff step out of persona. Say: "That was a simulation. The stress was real. Let's talk about it."

**2:47 – 2:49: Optional reveal.** "In the scenario file, about half of Brandywine's volume was repackaged. The Owner and Chef weren't in on it. Three small signals were on the table: Tess's label, the 14% price drop, the pork. And beef, which never changed price, was genuine."

**Questions (use 4; about 2.5 minutes each)**

1. **Decisions with incomplete data.** "Nobody could prove anything by 5:00. How did you decide? What would have changed your mind, and what would it have cost to find out?"
2. **Interruptions.** "You were interrupted dozens of times. What did you do to avoid losing the plan? What would you do differently in a real incident?"
3. **The traps.** "Lena offered a quiet edit. Teague offered silence. Someone offered a cover story. How did your team discuss that? Was it easy to say no?"
4. **The words on the record.** "You heard your statement read aloud next to Teague's. What did you learn about writing something that will be printed?"
5. **Contracts and constraints.** "Who read the contract? What did it let you do? How often in real jobs does the 'fine print' determine the options?"

**2:57 – 3:00: After-action review.** Each team calls out one thing to **sustain** and one to **improve.** Close.
