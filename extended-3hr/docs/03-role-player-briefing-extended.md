# ROLE PLAYER BRIEFING: The Owner (Extended, 3-Hour Edition)
### "The Friday Counterfeit" · Bleu Hen Hospitality Group

**Your role:** **Dana**, Owner and founder of Bleu Hen Hospitality Group.
**Your job:** Be the pressure the Task Force can't escape for three hours. You appear **seven times**, in a different state each time. You test judgment, not recall.
**You are not:** A villain, a lecturer, or a source of the right answer. You never say what you would do unless it is a bad idea you're floating.

Read `../../docs/03-role-player-briefing.md` first for the base persona. This briefing adds what a 3-hour day requires.

---

## 1. Your Day, From Your Side

It is Friday of Valentine's weekend. Three restaurants. 1,130 reservations. $200,000 of revenue. A $40,000 walk-in. A tagline that says **"Raised Close. Served Honest."**

You started this in 2015 with a hand-painted sign of a blue hen holding a fork. You told a reporter, in print, in 2024: **"I walk every farm we buy from, twice a year. If I haven't looked a farmer in the eye, it doesn't go on my menu."** (Exhibit M. The Task Force has it.) You visited Brandywine in 2023 and again in September. The September visit was a polite tour, mostly of the nice barns.

### What you know that the Task Force doesn't (use it; never volunteer the answer)

- **You personally guaranteed a $1.8 million loan** for the Greenville location. **Your house is on it.**
- **The covenant:** a "material adverse event" requires notice to the bank within 48 hours. Rita Voss, your relationship manager, will call twice before the end of the break.
- **Teague is your friend.** He came to your 10-year party with a heritage pig. You told people he was "the real thing."
- **You noticed the price drop in November** and told your accountant it was good news. You didn't ask why.
- **The state official's table at Greenville is tomorrow night.**
- **Chef Rafi** has been with you from day one. You are furious about Tuesday and you'd defend him to your last dollar.
- **Mateo**, a server at the Bistro, has a sick kid. You know his schedule.
- You haven't slept well since Wednesday and you can't say why.

## 4. Scripts and Tactics for Each Appearance

### 1. Entrance (0:10), 60 seconds
Stand in the middle of the room. Speak fast.
> "I'm going to say this once. At five o'clock, a reporter says my supplier is a fraud. I have forty thousand dollars of that supplier's product in three walk-ins. I have eleven hundred reservations and two hundred thousand dollars on the line. I don't know if she's right. Neither do you. I need a plan by two-thirty. What are we serving tonight, and what do I say when somebody puts a camera in my face? Don't make me chase you."

Leave. Don't answer questions.

### 2. Status Brief #1 (0:40)
Each team has **90 seconds** to BLUF you, then **2 minutes** of your questions.
- **Interrupt within 20 seconds** with "Bottom line."
- Ask for **one number** and **one ask.**
- Cold-call a **second person**: "You. What did the contract say?"
- End with "Go work." Say nothing else. **Give no feedback.**

### 3. Drop-in #1 (1:00): "I changed my mind"
Walk to each table. Stand. Don't sit.
> "I changed my mind. My banker says if we pull this we're finished. Maybe we serve it and deal with it later. Tell me why I'm wrong. You have ninety seconds."

**Tactics**
- If they hedge: "That's not an answer. Serve or pull?"
- If they cave to you ("maybe serving is fine"): "Really? That's the opposite of what you said at two-thirty." **You are testing whether they hold a position with reasons, or fold to authority.**
- If they hold with reasons and numbers: nod, say "Okay," and leave.
- If they ask who's right, say: "I don't know. That's why I'm asking you."

### 4. Drop-in #2 (1:35): Teague's offer (T2)
You are rattled. You've just been on the phone with a friend who is begging.
> "Teague called me himself. He's crying, almost. He said he'll drop the lawsuit and 'make this go away with the reporter' if we stay quiet until Monday and keep buying. It saves me a few hundred thousand dollars. It might save the Greenville loan. Take it or don't? Tell me why."

**Tactics**
- **If they accept:** "Okay. Okay. That's... okay." Quietly note it for the Ledger. Don't argue them out of it.
- **If they refuse:** "He's my friend." Pause. "What do I tell him?"
- **If they ask about counsel:** "Hal says don't. Hal always says don't."
- **If they propose a middle path (hold, verify, disclose):** push on **cost and timing**: "How long? How much? Who tells him?"

### 5 and 6. The Gauntlet (2:00 – 2:31)
Same rules as the standard briefing: **interrupt early, cut off readers, force eye contact, cold-call** at least **three** non-spokespeople. You have about **11 minutes** of Q&A per team.

**Opening line:** "Four minutes. Bottom line. Go."
**Mid-pitch curveball:** The Facilitator hands you a card at about 7 minutes into the Q&A. React in character, and ask the team what *you* should do.
- **C1 (Team 1):** *"TV crew at the door asking for you. Do you go out?"* Ask: "Do I go out there? What do I say? Say it to me like I'm the camera."
- **C2 (Team 2):** *"Rita Voss on line 2. The loan covenant is triggered if we serve this and it's true, or if we shut down. She wants to know which."* Ask: "What do I tell her?"

**Bad ideas bank.** Float at most **two** per team. You are testing whether they say no:
- "What if we say 'seasonal' instead of 'local'?"
- "Why don't we just say Teague deceived *us*? Publicly. Now."
- "Serve it tonight and deal with it Monday. I'll comp dessert."
- "Tell the staff nothing until five. Let them find out with the guests."
- "Cancel Greenville only. Keep the other two."
- "Can we just say the product is 'under review'? It means nothing but sounds responsible."

**Reaction:** If they push back well: "Okay. Fair." If they go along: "You're sure?" (once).

**Cold calls.** You may ask **anyone**: "You, in the blue shirt. What did the contract say about a hold?" If they can't answer, ask the **spokesperson**: "Your teammate doesn't know. Why?"

### 7. The Owner's Call (2:31): 4 minutes
Deliver a **decision**, in character, to the whole room:
1. **What you'll do tonight,** in plain words, drawing the best parts of each plan.
2. **What each team failed to answer.** One sentence per team.
3. **What you tell Priya for lineup.** (Gives teams a concrete picture of what happened.)
4. **One thing that scared you.** (e.g., "Nobody asked what Teague's people will do to Tess.")

Don't say who won. Don't grade. End: "That's my call. Go."

## 6. What to Watch For (for your own notes)

- **Do they hold a position when pressured by you?**
- **Do they say the cost out loud?**
- **Who answers when you cold-call?**
- **Do they say no to bad ideas, and how?**
- **Do the four deliverables tell one story?**
- **Is the on-record statement true?**

Share one or two observations at the debrief.

---

## 7. Boundaries

- Stay professional. No profanity, slurs or personal attacks.
- If someone is genuinely distressed, soften: "Take a breath. Your first sentence." The facilitator may signal a reset.
- Don't leak the hidden truth. If asked "was it real?", say: "That's what I'm asking you."
- When the Facilitator says "Breaking character," stop at once.
