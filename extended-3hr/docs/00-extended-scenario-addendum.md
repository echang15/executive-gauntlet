## 4. The Brandywine Master Supply Agreement (the fine print)

Effective **August 1, 2024**. Initial term through **July 31, 2027**. (18 months remain.)

| § | Clause | What it says in plain English |
|---|---|---|
| **2** | Exclusivity and minimums | Bleu Hen buys all proteins and seafood from Brandywine. Minimum **$1.1 million/year**. |
| **5** | Origin and labeling warranty | Brandywine warrants every product is **as labeled** and originates within **75 miles** of Buyer's restaurants. |
| **9.4** | **Verification hold** | If Buyer has "reasonable grounds to question the origin or labeling of any lot," Buyer may place that lot **on verification hold for up to 72 hours** on **written notice**, with **no breach and no penalty**. Supplier must produce chain-of-custody documents within 48 hours. |
| **9.5** | Audit | Buyer may audit Supplier's records on 5 business days' notice, or **24 hours "for cause."** |
| **11** | Termination for cause | Material breach allows termination after a 30-day cure period. **Willful mislabeling allows immediate termination.** |
| **12** | Liquidated damages | Termination **other than for cause**: Buyer owes **25% of the remaining minimum purchases**. 18 months × ($1.1M / 12) = $1.65M. **25% = $412,500.** |
| **14** | Non-disparagement | Neither party may make public statements that are "materially false or misleading" about the other. **Carve-out: truthful statements, and statements made in good faith to protect customers.** |
| **15** | Indemnity | If Supplier breaches §5, Supplier covers Buyer's replacement product, refunds and reasonable legal fees. **It does not cover lost profits or reputational damage.** |

**What this means (facilitator only).** A careful reader finds a middle path: a written §9.4 verification hold is **not** "pulling" the product in a way that triggers §12 damages, and it does not require calling Brandywine a fraud. But it requires written notice, it only helps for lots with "reasonable grounds" (the Tuesday labels), and it does not save tonight's menu on its own. It is a tool, not a solution. Counsel will not point teams to it unless asked about contract rights directly.

## 7. The Evidence: What Is Really True (FACILITATOR ONLY)

Extends Section 4 of the base canon.

| Product | Truth | Evidence a team can surface |
|---|---|---|
| **Chicken** | **Mostly counterfeit.** Repackaged from a North Carolina industrial plant. | Field check: **11 of 14** Tuesday cases have a second label, "Plant NC-4471". |
| **Pork** | **Mixed.** About 40% from a Pennsylvania plant. | Field check: **3 of 8** cases carry "Plant PA-2290". Marbling is off (Chef). |
| **Flounder** | **Likely not Delaware Bay.** Imported, thawed, boxed as "dayboat." | **No label either way.** Boxes show no catch date. Captain Dunn will say Delaware Bay flounder is "scarce this week." |
| **Rockfish** | Genuine, but from a different bay boat than listed. | Nothing definitive. |
| **Beef** | **Genuine.** | Kestrel Ridge tags; Chef met the rancher; invoice price **did not drop** in November. |

**The price signal:** In November, every protein except beef dropped about **14%** (see Exhibit H).

**What Teague will admit (only if pressed with plant codes):** "Limited co-packing at peak harvest. Standard in the industry. Everything is still from our farms." This is a partial, self-serving admission, not a confession.

**The field check is real but limited.** Tess can only inspect **Riverfront's Tuesday delivery**. It proves the labels exist, **not** that Brandywine intended fraud. Counsel: "That's evidence, not proof. But it's 'reasonable grounds' under §9.4."

## 8. Insurance

Policy has **no recall, contaminated-product or supplier-fraud endorsement.** Spoilage coverage applies only to equipment failure or power loss. "Reputational harm" is excluded. Gil Reyes: "I'd be lying if I said I thought this was covered. Talk to Hal about a claim against Brandywine under §15."
